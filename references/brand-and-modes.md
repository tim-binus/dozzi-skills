# Brand and shot-specific direction

## Shared DOZZI language

“A little less light. A little more you.” Warm, restful, human, understated. Product-led photography with believable comfort, not medical imagery or a cold tech aesthetic. Cream, oatmeal, ivory and warm taupe surround the product; textured charcoal remains readable. Cream palette is a direction, not an instruction to bleach skin or suppress natural skin tones.

Use an accepted product-only shot as the series' white-balance/exposure anchor. Light should have a consistent direction, softness and warmth. Skin, mask, hair, clothing and background must share that light and its shadows. Natural contact shadows, pillow compression, fabric folds, edge occlusion and depth are what make one physical photoshoot believable. Never a cut-out subject on a separately lit stock bedroom.

No five unrelated grades, wardrobes, environments or model identities in one collection. Do not combine the darker warm home campaign and the bright cream PDP studio campaign indiscriminately. Choose the mode's anchor and carry its grade through. Avoid sepia/yellow casts, glossy CGI, cinematic teal/orange and heavy beauty smoothing.

## Product-only / variant thumbnails

Apricot and Black have matching camera angle, product scale, floor, background and shadow direction. Keep one simple warm neutral surface and a physical resting contact shadow. No supplier white-background cutout aesthetic, floating mask or obstructive props. Product fill must read at small thumbnail size.

Bundles: 2 Black; 2 Apricot; 1 Apricot + 1 Black; 3 Black; 3 Apricot; 2 Apricot + 1 Black; 1 Apricot + 2 Black. Count **front mask bodies**, not folded straps. Overlap may create depth but cannot conceal the count or colour composition. File names contain historical typos: “2x apricot” is not reliable proof of pictured colour; visually check all bundle references.

Comfortable smile or natural visible teeth for awake/posed shots; gentle resting expression for lying poses. Hands may softly adjust the mask; do not force identical smiles or gestures across all images. Reference competitors only for broad pose/crop ideas when asked, never their branding or product construction.

## USP / product features

One visible feature per frame: nose panel, soft cotton, quiet closure, fit/stretch, lightness or portability. Default Apricot for standalone feature showcases, unless the brief specifies another variant. Fine material detail and plausible hand interaction are more useful than decorative graphics.

For holding: real finger/thumb opposition, support at a plausible point, gravity-induced drape, pressure folds and contact shadows. It is acceptable for fingers to obscure part of the logo. A pocket needs an opening, thickness, seams and believable garment volume; the mask should enter it rather than float over a flat fabric patch.

## Who it's for / relatable avatars

Each frame has a recognisable situation, not just a demographic label. Established five: post-shift daylight sleeper, every-small-light sensitivity, partner phone light, city/apartment spill light, and airplane traveller. Airplane is the user's chosen travel setting. Different people are allowed; use one shared photographic language and coherent colour grade. An avatar image can depict the problem without wearing/showing the product if that makes recognition stronger.

Some newly added library files (e.g. baby/cart) have no explicit acceptance in the recorded workflow. Treat them as candidates, not automatically approved additions or medical claims.

## Lifestyle / email

Prefer lived use: window-side wind-down, resting on pillows, leaning on a partner, travel packing. For email, the user explicitly chose lifestyle references instead of the PDP hero gallery. Keep one calm image that supports the message. Preserve full product/face framing; prefer compact image + text in the email rather than a full-width long portrait. Do not generate new imagery if a suitable supplied image already exists.

## Casual phone-style / UGC-like creative

Separate art direction: plausible phone perspective, imperfect crop, small exposure/white-balance irregularities, lived-in background and relaxed posture. The goal is casual photography, not polished catalogue work with simulated noise sprinkled over it. Avoid deliberate logo-showing poses and hands parked behind the head. Product construction still must be correct.

The supplied casual examples are synthetic creative references. Do not claim a real buyer took them, attach fabricated reviews/names/purchase histories, or call them verified customer evidence. If authentic testimonials are requested, use authentic supplied material.
